**Ether**, or **ETH** for short, is Ethereum's native cryptocurrency. If Ethereum is a giant machine, ETH is the fuel that keeps it running.

ETH was born with Ethereum in 2015. No company, organization or government controls ETH.

## What is ETH used for?

| Use | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Paying transaction fees** | Every time you send money or use an app on Ethereum, you pay a small fee in ETH, called a [gas fee](./gas-fees.mdx). |
| **Securing the network** | Validators lock up ETH as a stake to earn the right to process transactions. Doing the job right earns rewards; cheating gets penalized. |
| **Payments** | You can send ETH to anyone, anywhere, at any time, usually in just a few seconds. |
| **Collateral** | Many decentralized finance (DeFi) apps let you use ETH to borrow or lend. |

<Callout type="warn" title="Important">
To send anything on Ethereum, including stablecoins like USDT or USDC, your wallet needs a little ETH to pay the fee. A wallet with 100 USDT but no ETH can't move that USDT.
</Callout>

## Units of ETH

ETH can be divided into very small amounts. Two units you'll often see:

| Unit | Equals | Where you'll see it |
|---|---|---|
| **gwei** | 0.000000001 ETH (one billionth of an ETH) | Gas prices in your wallet |
| **wei** | 0.000000000000000001 ETH (the smallest unit) | Source code, developer tools |

For example: 1 ETH = 1,000,000,000 gwei.

## Sending and receiving ETH

**To receive ETH**, give the sender your **address**: a 42-character string starting with `0x`, or a QR code of that address.

**To send ETH**, open your wallet, enter the recipient's address and the amount, review the fee, then confirm.

- Ethereum creates a new block every 12 seconds, so transactions are usually recorded within 30 seconds.
- After about 15 minutes, the transaction reaches **finality** and can no longer be reversed. See [why](../how-it-works/blocks.mdx#when-can-a-block-no-longer-be-reversed).
- When the network is busy, you can pay a higher fee to get processed sooner.

<Callout type="warn" title="Warning">
Once a transaction is sent, it can't be canceled or recalled. Send to the wrong address and the money is gone. Check the address carefully before you hit send, and send a small test amount first if it's your first time sending to that address.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info" title="Note">
Ethereum is a public ledger. Anyone who knows your address can see your balance and transaction history. They can't take your money, but think before you share your address.
</Callout>

## ETH supply

Bitcoin has a maximum of 21 million coins. ETH has no fixed cap. Instead, its supply changes through two opposing mechanisms:

- **Issuance:** new ETH is created to reward validators, at a rate set by the protocol.
- **Burning:** since 2021, most of each transaction's fee (the base fee) is **burned**, meaning it's permanently removed from the supply.

<SupplyScene />

When the network is busy, more ETH can be burned than is issued, so the total supply shrinks. When the network is quiet, the total supply grows slightly.

The total supply and the amount of ETH burned are public. You can track them on sites like [Etherscan](https://etherscan.io) or [ultrasound.money](https://ultrasound.money).

## What is wrapped ETH (WETH)?

Many apps only work with tokens that follow the **ERC-20** standard, but ETH itself isn't an ERC-20 token. **WETH** (wrapped ETH) solves this: you deposit ETH into a smart contract and get back exactly the same amount of WETH, at a 1:1 ratio.

You can swap WETH back to ETH at any time. Most apps do this for you automatically.

## How do you get ETH?

- **Receive it:** someone you know sends it to you, or you accept payment in ETH.
- **Buy it:** on an exchange or directly in some wallet apps.
- **Earn it:** for example, by staking ETH you already have to earn rewards.
