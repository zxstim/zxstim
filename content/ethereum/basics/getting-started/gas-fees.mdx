Every time you send money or use an app on Ethereum, you pay a small fee in ETH. This fee is called a **gas fee**.

## Summary

- Every transaction costs a gas fee, paid in ETH.
- Fees aren't fixed: the busier the network, the higher the fee.
- The more complex the transaction, the more gas it uses.
- Layer 2 makes fees much cheaper.

## What is gas?

Picture Ethereum as a giant computer shared by the whole world. Every task you ask it to do, from sending money to running a smart contract, takes a certain amount of computing work. **Gas** is the unit that measures that work, like the liters of fuel a car trip needs.

The more complex the task, the more gas it uses:

| Transaction type | Gas used (estimate) |
|---|---|
| Send ETH | 21,000 |
| Send an ERC-20 token (like USDT or USDC) | about 65,000 |
| Transfer an NFT | about 85,000 |
| Swap tokens on Uniswap | about 185,000 |

## How is the fee calculated?

```
Fee = Gas used × (Base fee + Priority fee)
```

- **Base fee**: the minimum fee, set automatically by the network and adjusted after every block depending on how busy it is. This part is **burned**, meaning it's permanently destroyed.
- **Priority fee**: a "tip" to the validator so your transaction gets processed sooner. This part goes to the validator.

Gas prices are usually quoted in **gwei**. 1 gwei is one billionth of an ETH.

**Example:** sending ETH uses 21,000 gas. If the base fee is 1 gwei and the priority fee is 0.5 gwei:

```
21,000 × (1 + 0.5) gwei = 31,500 gwei = 0.0000315 ETH
```

You don't need to do the math yourself. Your wallet estimates the fee and shows it before you confirm. Many wallets let you choose **Slow**, **Normal** or **Fast**: pay more to get processed sooner.

## Why do fees go up and down?

Each block can only hold a limited amount of computation, called the block's **gas limit**. The network aims for each block to use half of that limit, and adjusts the base fee after every block to stay on target:

- If a block uses **more** than the target, the next block's base fee **goes up**. A block filled to the limit raises the fee by 12.5%, the largest possible increase.
- If a block uses **less** than the target, the base fee **goes down**, by up to 12.5%.
- If a block uses exactly the target, the fee stays the same.

When lots of people want to transact at once, for example when a popular NFT project opens its sale or the market swings sharply, blocks keep filling up and the base fee climbs block after block. High fees make people who aren't in a hurry wait, blocks become less full, and fees come back down.

Gas prices can change after every block, that is, every 12 seconds. Because the next block's base fee can be calculated in advance from the current block, wallets can estimate fees quite accurately.

## Why are gas fees needed?

- **Preventing spam:** if transactions were free, bad actors could send millions of junk transactions and paralyze the network.
- **Limiting load:** each block has a gas limit, so the network never gets overloaded.
- **Paying for the work:** the priority fee goes to validators, the people who keep the network running.

## How can you pay less in fees?

1. **Use Layer 2.** Networks like Arbitrum, Optimism or Base usually charge under $0.01 for a simple transaction.
2. **Wait for a quiet time.** If it's not urgent, wait a few minutes or a few hours. Ethereum is often cheaper when it's nighttime in North America, which is around noon to afternoon Vietnam time.
3. **Don't pick "Fast" when you don't need it.** "Slow" or "Normal" still gets processed; it just takes a little longer.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I have to pay fees in ETH?**
Yes. On Ethereum, gas fees are always paid in ETH, even when you send another token like USDT. Always keep a little ETH in your wallet.

**Do I lose the fee if a transaction fails?**
Yes. If a transaction fails partway through, for example because the gas limit was set too low or a contract rejected it, you're still charged for the gas already used. Wallets usually warn you in advance if a transaction is likely to fail.

**Who receives my gas fee?**
The base fee is burned, so no one receives it. The priority fee goes to the validator that included your transaction in a block.
