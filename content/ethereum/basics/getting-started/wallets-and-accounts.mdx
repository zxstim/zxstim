To use Ethereum, you need a **wallet**. But an Ethereum wallet differs from the wallet in your pocket in one important way: **a wallet doesn't hold money**. Your assets live on the Ethereum network. The wallet is just a tool that lets you see and use them.

## Four concepts to tell apart

Compare them with email:

| Concept | Like | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| **Address** | Email address | A 42-character string starting with `0x`. You give it to others so they can send you money. Share it freely. |
| **Private key** | Email password | Used to **sign** transactions, proving you own the account. Keep it absolutely secret. |
| **Account** | Mailbox | A key pair: a public key (which produces the address) and a private key. Assets belong to the account. |
| **Wallet** | Email app | An app that uses your keys to show balances, send transactions and sign in to apps. |

An Ethereum address looks like this (an example address, don't send money to it):

```
0x5859c049b26bd3882f00a3bbedebba5113abaaf2
```

Because a wallet is just a "reader app," you can switch to another wallet at any time and keep the same accounts and assets. One wallet can also manage many accounts.

## Recovery phrase

When you create a new wallet, it gives you a **recovery phrase** (also called a seed phrase): 12 or 24 English words in a specific order. For example:

```
there aeroplane curve vent formation doge possible product distinct under spirit lamp
```

From this phrase, the wallet generates the private keys for all your accounts. That means:

- **Whoever has your recovery phrase has all your assets.** They don't need your password or your phone.
- **Lose your recovery phrase and your device, and you lose your assets forever.** No one can recover them for you.
- **With your recovery phrase, you can get your assets back** in any wallet that supports it, even if your phone is lost or broken.

The recovery phrase produces the private key, and the key produces the address, so anyone who enters the same 12 words gets that same address back, whether it's you or a thief. <Callout type="warn" title="Warning">
How to keep your recovery phrase:
- Write it on paper (or engrave it in metal) and store it somewhere safe. You can keep two copies in two different places.
- **Don't** take a screenshot, **don't** save it in phone notes, email, Google Drive or iCloud, and don't send it over Zalo, Messenger or Telegram.
- **Never** enter your recovery phrase on a website or give it to anyone, including people who claim to be "tech support."
</Callout>

## Types of wallets

| Wallet type | Pros | Keep in mind |
|---|---|---|
| **Mobile wallet** | Convenient, always with you, easy QR scanning | Risky if your phone is lost or infected with malware |
| **Browser extension wallet** | Connects quickly to web apps | Install the official extension, and watch out for fake ones |
| **Web wallet** | Nothing to install, just open your browser | Always go to the correct site address; bookmark it |
| **Desktop wallet** | A good fit if you use a computer a lot | Your computer must be free of malware |
| **Hardware wallet** | Safest: keys live on a separate device that never goes online | Costs money to buy the device; only buy from the official manufacturer |

For large amounts, a hardware wallet is the safest choice.

## Hold it yourself or leave it on an exchange?

When you buy crypto on an exchange, the exchange usually holds the assets for you. You log in with a username and password, and if you forget them, the exchange can recover your account. In return, you have to trust the exchange: if it gets hacked, runs into financial trouble or locks your account, your assets are at risk.

When you use your own wallet, you're in **self-custody**: only you control your assets, and no one can freeze them. But you're also responsible for keeping your recovery phrase safe.

## Create your first Ethereum account

You don't need to sign up anywhere. The wallet creates the account for you.

1. **Choose a wallet.** Use the table above to pick the right type.
2. **Download the wallet from an official source:** the wallet's website, the App Store or Google Play. Check the developer's name carefully, because there are many fake apps.
3. **Open the wallet and choose "Create new wallet."** The wallet will ask you to set a password. This password only unlocks the wallet on that device; it's not the same as your recovery phrase.
4. **Write your recovery phrase down on paper**, in the correct order. Many wallets will ask you to re-enter a few of the words to make sure you copied it correctly.
5. **Test with a small amount first.** Receive a little ETH and try sending some before you use large amounts.

Done. The wallet will show your address. That's your Ethereum account.

## One address, many networks

Besides Ethereum, there are many compatible networks, such as the [Layer 2](../how-it-works/layer-2.mdx) networks Arbitrum, Optimism and Base, or other chains like BNB Chain. The same key gives you the same address on all of them, but each network keeps its own record of balances.

<NetworksScene />

- **Your wallet only shows the network you've selected.** 10 USDC on Base won't show up while your wallet is on Ethereum. The money isn't lost; it's just on another network.
- **Moving between networks requires a bridge.** A regular send on one network only moves money within that network.
- **When sending, the sender and recipient must use the same network.** This matters most when depositing to an exchange: pick exactly the network the exchange specifies. If you pick the wrong network, the money arrives at the right address but on a network the exchange doesn't monitor, and it may be lost.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use my Ethereum address on other networks?**
Yes, on Ethereum-compatible networks: you have the same address on all of them, but your assets on each network are separate. See [One address, many networks](#one-address-many-networks).

**Can I send bitcoin to an Ethereum address?**
No. Bitcoin and Ethereum are separate networks with different address formats. If you send to the wrong one, you lose the money.

**I lost my phone. Do I need to reinstall the same wallet?**
Not necessarily. Most wallets use the same recovery phrase standard (BIP-39), so you can enter your recovery phrase into a different wallet and get your account back.

**Can I use one account on several devices?**
Yes, by entering the same recovery phrase into a wallet on each device. But the more devices hold your keys, the higher the risk.
