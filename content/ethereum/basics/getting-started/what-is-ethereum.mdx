**Ethereum** is an open, public **blockchain** network that launched in July 2015. Its native currency is **ether**, or **ETH** for short.

Bitcoin lets you send and receive digital money. Ethereum does that and more: it can run programs called **smart contracts**. This means anyone can create digital assets and apps that run nonstop, worldwide, without asking anyone's permission.

## Summary

- Ethereum is a network of thousands of independent computers around the world. No company or government owns it.
- ETH is used to pay transaction fees and to secure the network.
- Smart contracts let Ethereum run apps: finance, stablecoins, NFTs, games, social networks…
- All you need to get started is a wallet and an internet connection.

## The Ethereum network

Think of Ethereum as shared digital infrastructure for the whole world: anyone can use it, but no one can change it on their own.

The network is made up of thousands of computers called **nodes**, run by ordinary people in many countries. Each node keeps a copy of Ethereum's data, and together they check every transaction. Compared with a system owned by a single organization, this design has three advantages:

| Advantage | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Censorship resistant** | No bank or company can block you or freeze your account. Every valid transaction is treated the same way, because the rules live in the code. |
| **Secure** | Apps are protected by the network itself. To change a contract, an attacker would have to take over most of the network, which would be enormously expensive and practically impossible. |
| **Always on** | If some nodes stop running because of software bugs, natural disasters or being blocked, the rest keep the network going. |

### Layer 2: express lanes

When many people use it at once, transaction fees on Ethereum can rise. So many teams have built **Layer 2** networks that run on top of Ethereum, such as Arbitrum, Optimism, Base and ZKsync.

Think of Ethereum as the main highway and Layer 2s as express lanes running alongside it: transactions are faster and fees are often under $0.01, while still relying on Ethereum's security.

## Ether (ETH)

ETH is Ethereum's native cryptocurrency. You use ETH to:

- pay **gas fees** whenever you send money or use an app,
- **stake** it to help secure the network and earn rewards,
- make payments, sending it to anyone, anywhere, in seconds.

## How does Ethereum work?

When it launched in 2015, Ethereum used **proof of work**, like Bitcoin: computers burned huge amounts of electricity to solve a puzzle, and whichever solved it first got to add the next block of transactions and earn ETH as a reward.

In 2022, Ethereum switched to **proof of stake**, cutting its electricity use by more than 99.9%. Instead of solving puzzles, **validators** lock up their ETH as a stake to earn the right to process transactions. Do the job correctly and they earn ETH. Cheat and they lose part of their stake.

### Example: you send $10 in stablecoins to a friend

1. You open your wallet, enter your friend's address and the amount, then tap send.
2. Your wallet **signs** the transaction with your key and sends it to the network.
3. The transaction waits in a public queue called the **mempool**.
4. Every 12 seconds, a validator is chosen to create a new block. That validator puts your transaction in the block and earns a small fee.
5. The stablecoin's smart contract moves $10 from you to your friend. Both wallets' balances are updated.
6. Thousands of other validators check the block and **attest** to it. After about 15 minutes, the transaction reaches **finality**: it can no longer be reversed.

Every change is synced across thousands of independent nodes. That's why Ethereum is reliable, transparent and hard to censor.

## What is Ethereum used for?

**For everyday users**, all you need is a wallet and the internet to:

- use financial services without a bank account or credit history,
- own digital assets that can't be copied or confiscated,
- sign in to apps with your wallet instead of an email and password,
- join global communities: vote, contribute and earn rewards, regardless of borders.

**For businesses and developers**, Ethereum is a platform with a global payment system built in from day one. For example, PayPal issues its PYUSD stablecoin on Ethereum. In 2025, Shopify integrated the Base network so shoppers can pay with stablecoins at millions of stores.

**For governments and organizations**, Ethereum makes distributing funds transparent. For example, Ukraine's Ministry of Digital Transformation used Ethereum to send wartime aid directly to people and to nongovernmental organizations.

## How is Ethereum different from Bitcoin?

Bitcoin and Ethereum are the world's two largest cryptocurrencies. Both let you send money without a bank, both run on a blockchain, and both are open to everyone. But they differ in several ways:

| | Bitcoin | Ethereum |
|---|---|---|
| **Role** | Like "digital gold": stores and moves value | A programmable platform: it runs apps too |
| **Programmability** | Very limited, deliberately, to stay simple and robust | Full smart contracts |
| **Consensus** | Proof of work (mining), uses a lot of electricity | Proof of stake (staking), energy efficient |
| **Supply** | Fixed at 21 million BTC | Flexible: new ETH is issued to reward validators, while part of the fees is burned |

In short: Bitcoin is a tool for moving value. Ethereum is a platform for building it.

## Who runs Ethereum?

No one. Ethereum has no CEO, no board of directors and no central authority. The network is maintained by many groups of people:

- developers, who write and propose upgrades,
- node operators, who make up the distributed infrastructure,
- people who stake ETH to validate transactions,
- the community, which builds tools and culture,
- and you, when you use the network.

The Ethereum Foundation still funds research and development, but it doesn't control the network. Every change starts as an **Ethereum Improvement Proposal** (EIP), is discussed in public and is only adopted if the community supports it. That's why Ethereum changes more slowly than a startup, but is very hard to shut down or take over.

### Key milestones

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 2013 | Vitalik Buterin, then 19, publishes the Ethereum whitepaper |
| 2014 | The Ethereum Foundation is founded and raises funds from the community |
| 2015 | The Ethereum network launches with the Frontier release |
| 2016 | A smart contract vulnerability causes The DAO to lose 3.6 million ETH, leading to a chain split |
| 2020 | The Beacon Chain launches, starting the move to proof of stake |
| 2021 | The London upgrade starts burning part of each transaction fee (EIP-1559) |
| 2022 | The Merge: Ethereum fully switches to proof of stake |
| 2025 | Pectra improves smart wallets and Layer 2. Fusaka (December) helps Layer 2s post data to Ethereum more efficiently |

The next upgrade, Glamsterdam, is being developed in the open. It has been running on the Sepolia testnet since October 2026, but it has no mainnet date yet.
