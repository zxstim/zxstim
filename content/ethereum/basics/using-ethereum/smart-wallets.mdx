The account you create with an ordinary wallet is controlled by a private key (an EOA, see [Addresses and keys](../how-it-works/addresses-and-keys.mdx)). As ethereum.org puts it: with the key you can do everything, without the key you can do nothing. Lose the key and you can't get it back; leak the key and a thief takes everything instantly.

**Smart wallets** (smart accounts) change that: your account gains the code of a [smart contract](../how-it-works/smart-contracts.mdx), so you can set rules for the account itself. This direction of development is called **account abstraction**.

## What can a smart wallet do?

- **Social recovery**: you pick a few people or devices you trust in advance. If you lose your phone, they confirm together to attach a new key to your account. No more losing everything because you lost one piece of paper.
- **Multisig**: large transactions need several keys to sign together, for example 2 out of 3 devices.
- **Spending limits and trusted lists:** only move up to a certain amount per day, or only send to addresses you have approved. Even a thief who gets your phone can't drain everything.
- **Batching:** approve spending and swap tokens in a single transaction, instead of signing twice.
- **Sponsored fees:** an app can pay the gas fee for you, or you can pay the fee in another token instead of ETH.
- **Passkeys:** sign with your fingerprint or face, with the key kept in your phone's secure chip. Since the Fusaka upgrade (December 2025), Ethereum supports this type of signature natively.

## Two ways to get a smart wallet

1. **A separate contract account** (following the ERC-4337 standard, live on mainnet since March 2023). You get a new address, which is itself a smart contract. All the rules live in that contract. See [ERC-4337](./erc-4337.mdx).
2. **Upgrading a regular account** (EIP-7702, since the Pectra upgrade in May 2025). You keep your old address and attach a contract's code to get batching, sponsored fees… The key point: **the original private key still has full control**. Attaching multisig wallet code to your account doesn't turn it into a real multisig wallet. If your old recovery phrase leaks, the new rules can't save you. See [EIP-7702](./eip-7702.mdx).

<Callout type="error" title="Caution">
Scammers may trick you into "upgrading your account" by attaching the code of a contract they wrote. Signing that is handing full control of your account to the scammer. Only upgrade through your own wallet app, never through an unfamiliar website or link.
</Callout>

## Coming soon

Frame transactions (EIP-8141) have been scheduled for the Hegotá upgrade. They could bring recovery, spending limits and sponsored fees straight into the protocol, and pave the way for quantum-safe signatures. As of November 2026, the proposal is still being finalized and Hegotá has no launch date yet, so this is still a plan.

## Summary

- Regular account: one key does everything, and losing the key means losing everything.
- Smart wallet: an account with its own rules, such as social recovery, multisig, spending limits, batching, sponsored fees and passkeys.
- Upgrading a regular account (EIP-7702) keeps your old address, but the old key still has full control. Only upgrade through your wallet app.
