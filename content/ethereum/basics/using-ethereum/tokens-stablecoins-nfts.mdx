Besides ETH, there are thousands of kinds of **tokens** on Ethereum: digital dollars, reward points, in-game items, shares, proofs of ownership. All of them are created and tracked by [smart contracts](../how-it-works/smart-contracts.mdx).

<TokenScene />

## Where do tokens live?

A token is a smart contract that keeps a **balance ledger**: which address holds how much. The most common standard is **ERC-20**, around since 2015, which defines shared functions such as checking a balance, transferring tokens, and allowing someone else to spend them.

- Tokens aren't inside your wallet. Your wallet reads the contract's balance ledger and shows it to you. What the wallet holds is your private key, the thing that lets you give orders to the contract.
- Transferring a token means sending a transaction to the token contract, asking it to subtract from your balance and add to the recipient's. The transaction fee is still paid in ETH.
- Sending tokens to the token contract's own address, or to a contract that doesn't know how to handle tokens, usually means they are lost for good. According to ethereum.org, more than 83 million USD worth of tokens had been lost this way as of mid-2024.

### Spending permissions

For an app (such as a token exchange) to use your tokens, you have to sign an **approval** (approve) that lets it spend them, along with a spending limit. If you give unlimited approval, that app can use all of your tokens of that kind, even after you've stopped using the app. If the app gets hacked, your tokens can be drained.

Only approve the exact amount you need, and revoke old permissions regularly. See [Reading a transaction](./reading-a-transaction.mdx#using-a-block-explorer-to-protect-yourself).

## Stablecoins

A **stablecoin** is a token designed to hold a steady price, usually 1 USD. Each type holds its price in a different way, with different risks:

| Type | Holds its price thanks to | You have to trust |
|---|---|---|
| Backed by fiat currency (such as USDC, USDT) | The issuer keeps real money or equivalent assets as reserves and redeems 1:1 | That the issuer really holds enough reserves, and is audited |
| Backed by crypto assets (such as DAI, USDS) | Crypto assets worth more than what is issued are locked up as collateral, for example 2 USD of assets for 1 USD of stablecoin | The smart contract, and the price of the collateral |
| Backed by precious metals | Gold or silver stored by the issuer | The issuer and its vault |
| Algorithmic | A mechanism that adjusts supply on its own, with no backing assets | The algorithm, and the market not losing confidence |

Things to know:

- **The price can slip.** When people doubt the reserves or the market panics, a stablecoin can fall below 1 USD.
- **The issuer has a lot of power.** With fiat-backed stablecoins such as USDC and USDT, the issuer can freeze the tokens at an address.
- **Pick the right network.** USDT or USDC may have the same name everywhere, but on each network (Ethereum, the Layer 2s, other chains) it is a separate token. Sending on the wrong network can lose you money.

## NFTs

An **NFT** (non-fungible token) is a token where every unit is unique. An NFT contract (following the ERC-721 standard, around since 2018, or ERC-1155) records who owns each ID number. Anyone can check that ownership.

- **The image usually isn't onchain.** The contract usually only holds a link to the description and the image, stored on a server or on a storage network such as IPFS. If that storage goes offline, the image can disappear, even though you still own the NFT.
- **Owning an NFT doesn't mean owning the copyright** to the work, unless the seller commits to that.
- **Royalties for the creator** when an NFT is resold can be written into the contract, but the Ethereum protocol doesn't enforce them: it's up to each marketplace whether to pay them.

## Wrapped tokens: WETH

ETH came before the ERC-20 standard, so it doesn't follow it. **WETH** (wrapped ETH) is ETH deposited into a contract in exchange for a matching ERC-20 token, 1:1. Hand back the WETH and you get your ETH back. Read more in [What is ether (ETH)?](../getting-started/what-is-ether.mdx#what-is-wrapped-eth-weth).

<Callout type="warn" title="Warning">
Anyone can create a token with any name and symbol, even one identical to a well-known token. Scammers also send fake tokens to your wallet themselves to lure you onto a scam site. Don't interact with unfamiliar tokens that show up in your wallet on their own. See [Staying safe](../getting-started/staying-safe.mdx).
</Callout>

## Summary

- A token is a balance ledger inside a smart contract. Your wallet only displays it; the private key is the thing you actually hold.
- Only let apps spend the exact amount they need, and revoke old permissions.
- Stablecoins hold their price thanks to backing assets, but you still have to trust the issuer or its mechanism.
- An NFT records ownership of an ID number; the image usually lives offchain.
