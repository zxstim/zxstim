Many people think crypto is anonymous. With Ethereum, it isn't. The chain doesn't record your name, only your address, so Ethereum is **pseudonymous**. But according to ethereum.org, simply analyzing how an address behaves can be enough to reveal sensitive information, and even to identify the person behind it.

<PrivacyScene />

## What anyone can see

By opening a [block explorer](./reading-a-transaction.mdx), anyone can see this about an address:

- its current balance and the tokens it holds,
- every payment received and sent, to whom, and when,
- every contract and app that address has ever used.

This data stays on the chain forever and can't be deleted.

## How does an address get linked to you?

- **Withdrawing from an exchange that verified your identity.** The exchange knows the receiving address is yours. The exchange's data can leak or be demanded by authorities.
- **Making it public yourself:** posting your address on social media, using an ENS name that matches your social media handle, receiving payments publicly.
- **Linking addresses together:** funding several addresses from the same source, or having them send money back and forth, is enough for an analyst to group them as belonging to one person. That's why using several addresses helps less than you might think.
- **Wallets and nodes know too.** The node your wallet asks (usually one run by a provider) sees your IP address, your wallet addresses and when you use them. See [Nodes](../how-it-works/nodes.mdx).

## Staying more private

- **Use separate addresses for separate purposes**, and don't fund them from the same source. This lowers the risk, but it doesn't make you anonymous.
- **Be careful about what you make public:** addresses, ENS names, screenshots of your wallet.
- **Use your own node**, or a node you trust, instead of your wallet's default node.
- **Privacy tools** are being developed, such as stealth addresses (one-time addresses, ERC-5564), privacy pools and privacy-focused Layer 2s. They come with trade-offs in fees and complexity, and every country has different rules: look into the regulations in force before you use them.

"Zero-knowledge" (ZK) technology doesn't automatically make everything private: the data posted onchain, your IP address or the apps you use can still give information away.

<Callout type="warn" title="Warning">
Don't show off your balance or an address holding a lot of money. Once other people know how much you have, criminals can track you down in real life to force you to transfer it. Cases like this are on the rise.
</Callout>

## Coming soon

Ethereum's roadmap aims for "private by default": reading data privately, sending transactions privately, and proving something without revealing the data. According to ethereum.org, none of these are finished yet.

## Summary

- Ethereum is pseudonymous: anyone can read every transaction of an address.
- Once an address is linked to your name, for example through an exchange, your whole history is exposed.
- Separate your addresses, be careful about what you make public, use your own node. None of these makes you fully anonymous.
