**Gas** is the unit that measures how much computation it takes to do something on Ethereum. It's like fuel for a car: a long trip burns more fuel, and running a complex program burns more gas.

The [Gas fees](../getting-started/gas-fees.mdx) page shows you how to pay less in fees. This page explains why Ethereum needs gas in the first place.

## Why does Ethereum need gas?

Ethereum is one computer shared by the whole world. That brings four problems, and gas solves all four:

1. **Every node has to rerun everything.** Each transaction is rerun by thousands of nodes to check it. Computation on Ethereum uses the resources of the whole network, so whoever asks for it has to pay.
2. **Stopping spam.** If transactions were free, bad actors could send millions of junk transactions and clog the network. With fees, spam gets very expensive.
3. **Stopping endless loops.** Ethereum can run any kind of program, including programs that never stop. In theory, there's no way to know in advance whether an arbitrary program will ever stop. Gas sets a ceiling: when the gas runs out, the program is stopped, so no one can freeze the network.
4. **Keeping blocks a reasonable size.** Each block has a gas limit, so an ordinary computer can still run a node.

## Gas units and gas price

There are two separate things here:

- **The amount of gas** depends on what you do. Each operation has a fixed gas cost: addition is cheap, while storing data permanently on the chain costs much more.
- **The price per unit of gas** depends on how busy the network is, and it's measured in **gwei**. 1 gwei is one billionth of an ETH.

| Action | Amount of gas (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Send ETH | 21,000 |
| Transfer an ERC-20 token | 65,000 |
| Transfer an NFT | 85,000 |
| Swap tokens on a decentralized exchange | 185,000 |

The fee you pay is the amount of gas used times the price per unit of gas. An illustrative example from ethereum.org: sending ETH uses 21,000 gas; with a base fee of 10 gwei and a priority fee of 2 gwei, the fee is 21,000 × 12 = 252,000 gwei, or 0.000252 ETH. Real prices change from block to block.

## Gas limit: you pay for what you use

When you send a transaction, your wallet sets a **gas limit**: the most gas you're willing to pay for.

- **You use less than the limit:** you only pay for what you used. Set a limit of 50,000 for an ETH transfer and it still uses only 21,000; the rest isn't charged.
- **The limit is below the minimum needed:** the transaction is rejected right away. It doesn't go into a block and costs nothing.
- **It runs out of gas partway through:** the transaction stops and **reverts** all its changes, but the gas already used is still lost. The computing work has already been done, so it still has to be paid for.

## The base fee is burned, the priority fee goes to the validator

Since the London upgrade (August 2021, EIP-1559), the fee has two parts:

- **Base fee**: set automatically by the protocol for each block. Each block has a target gas amount equal to half the limit. If the previous block was busier than the target, the base fee goes up; if it was quieter, the fee goes down, by at most 12.5% each time. The base fee is **burned**: it disappears forever, and no one receives it.
- **Priority fee**, or tip: paid to the validator that puts your transaction into a block. Without a tip, the validator has no reason to pick your transaction.

Your wallet also sets a **max fee** per unit of gas. You never pay more than this. If the base fee is lower when your transaction lands in a block, the difference isn't charged.

Because most of the fee is burned, when the network is busy, the amount of ETH burned can be larger than the amount of new ETH issued to validators.

<Callout type="info" title="Note">
Fees on Ethereum are always paid in ETH, even when you're sending another token. Keep a little ETH in your wallet to pay fees. The switch to proof of stake in 2022 didn't make fees cheaper. What makes fees cheaper is [Layer 2](./layer-2.mdx), where rollups pay a separate kind of gas for blob data.
</Callout>

## Summary

- Gas measures computation. Every node has to rerun each transaction, so computation has to be paid for.
- Gas stops spam, stops endless loops and keeps blocks a reasonable size.
- The fee is the gas used times the gas price. The base fee is burned; the priority fee goes to the validator.
- If a transaction runs out of gas partway through, it's reverted, but the fee is still lost.
