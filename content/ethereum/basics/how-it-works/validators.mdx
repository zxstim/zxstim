**Validators** are the people who keep Ethereum secure. They're the "generals" in the [consensus](./consensus.mdx) problem: they propose new blocks, check other people's blocks and vote. To get that job, they have to stake ETH.

## You have to stake to take part

To run a validator, you lock at least **32 ETH** into Ethereum's deposit contract. The reason is simple: anyone who votes must have something to lose if they cheat. No stake, no vote.

- **Why 32 ETH and not less?** The lower the minimum, the more validators there are, the more votes have to be passed back and forth every slot, and the more powerful the machines running nodes have to be.
- **What's the maximum?** Since the Pectra upgrade (May 2025), a single validator can hold up to 2,048 ETH. People with a lot of ETH no longer need to run dozens of separate validators.
- **How long before it starts?** After depositing the stake, a validator has to wait in an activation queue, anywhere from a few hours to a few weeks depending on how many others are waiting.

Running a node just to check the blockchain yourself doesn't need any ETH. You only need a stake if you want to vote.

## What do validators do?

- **Propose blocks.** In each 12-second slot, one validator is chosen at random to propose a block. The bigger the stake, the more often a validator gets chosen.
- **Vote.** Every epoch (6.4 minutes), each active validator casts one **attestation** vote for the head of the chain and for the epoch's checkpoint. The voting is split among small **committees**, one committee per slot.

## Rewards and penalties

- **Doing the job right earns rewards.** Voting on time and proposing blocks earns newly issued ETH, plus tips from transaction fees. The reward rate is the same for everyone, whether you're rich or poor.
- **Being absent costs a little.** If your machine is off, you lose roughly what you would have earned during that time. Staying active for about the same length of time makes it back. Being absent isn't cheating.
- **Cheating gets your stake slashed.** Three kinds of misbehavior count as cheating, for example signing two different blocks for the same slot. A validator that breaks the rules immediately loses a small part of its stake, then is forced to leave the network after 36 days. If it's the only one breaking the rules, the total loss is usually under 0.1%. If a large number of validators break the rules at the same time, as in an organized attack, they can lose up to their entire stake.

Most slashings so far have come from carelessness: running the same key on two machines at once, which makes that key sign twice.

## What are the ways to stake?

| Method | How much you need | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|
| Solo staking at home | 32 ETH | You hold your own keys, get the full reward and do the most to keep the network decentralized. You have to keep your machine running reliably yourself |
| Running a node with collateral | About 1.5 to 4 ETH | You run the machine yourself; the rest of the stake is pooled from other people through a protocol |
| Delegating to a service | 32 ETH | The service runs the machine and holds the signing key. You keep the right to withdraw, but you have to trust the service |
| Pooled staking, liquid staking | From 0.01 ETH | Pools many people's money through smart contracts. There's the risk of contract bugs and of concentration in a few operators |
| Through an exchange | Any amount | The easiest, but you have to trust the exchange completely, and it helps the network the least |

<Callout type="error" title="Caution">
This isn't investment advice. Every way of staking carries risk, and rewards aren't guaranteed. Be wary of anyone promising unusually high returns for "staking ETH": it's usually a scam.
</Callout>

## How global is Ethereum?

No authority counts the exact number of nodes, because anyone can run one without registering. Crawlers only see nodes that accept connections from outside, so the real number is even higher. According to [Ethernodes](https://ethernodes.org/countries), with data viewed on November 8, 2026:

- It found **8,717 execution-layer nodes** in **95 countries and territories**, and 4,886 consensus-layer nodes in 72 countries and territories.
- There are nodes on every continent, from the US, Germany, Japan and Singapore to Brazil, South Africa and Australia. Vietnam has 24 nodes.
- The US has the most (about 30%), followed by Germany (about 14%), then the UK, Finland and France at about 4% each.

Because the network is spread out like this, no single country can shut Ethereum down on its own. As long as validators holding 2/3 of the stake stay online, the chain keeps reaching finality. If too many validators stop running at the same time, Ethereum gradually drains the stake of those who are absent until the ones still online make up 2/3 again.

But the decentralization isn't perfect, and the community is keeping an eye on two risks:

- **Concentration in where machines are located.** Nearly half of all nodes are in two countries, and many nodes rent machines from a handful of big cloud providers. People running nodes at home help the network depend less on those places.
- **Concentration in software.** Each node runs one of several clients written by different teams. If one client makes up more than 2/3 of the network and has a bug, the chain could go the wrong way. The goal is for no client to have more than 1/3. See the current shares at [clientdiversity.org](https://clientdiversity.org).

## Summary

- To be a validator, you have to stake at least 32 ETH. The stake is what you lose if you cheat.
- Doing the job right earns rewards, being absent costs a little, and cheating gets your stake slashed and you removed.
- Ethereum nodes run in about 95 countries and territories, but they're still heavily concentrated in the US and Germany.
