A [Layer 2](../basics/how-it-works/layer-2.mdx) runs transactions outside Ethereum to make them fast and cheap, then sends data and proofs back to Ethereum. For businesses, this is the most common way to use Ethereum today:

- **Cheap and fast.** Fees are usually just a small fraction of those on Ethereum. Since November 5, 2026, Base produces a block every 200 milliseconds.
- **Still anchored to Ethereum.** The final results are recorded on Ethereum, so the network inherits Ethereum's [neutrality](./neutral-blockspace.mdx) and [verifiability](./verifiability.mdx), to a degree that depends on each network's design.
- **No separate token needed for security.** A Layer 2 pays its fees to Ethereum in ETH. Its security comes from Ethereum, not from a new token.

## Share or run your own

| | Use an existing Layer 2 | Run your own Layer 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Getting started | Deploy your contracts and you're done | You have to set up and operate a whole network |
| Users, liquidity | Already there, shared | You have to attract them yourself |
| Customization | Follows that network's rules | You can choose the fee token, who may take part, where data is stored… See [Customizing a Layer 2](./customizing-layer-2.mdx) |
| Transaction fees | Paid to the network operator | Yours, minus what you pay to Ethereum |
| Responsibility | The network operator's | Yours: running the sequencer 24/7, security, upgrades |
| Who has to trust whom | You trust the operator | Customers and partners trust you |

According to ethereum.org, a dedicated network can go live within a few weeks thanks to ready-made stacks. The real timeline depends heavily on your security and compliance requirements.

## Who already runs their own Layer 2

Data from ETHBEAT (formerly L2BEAT), November 8, 2026.

| Network | Company | Stack | Mainnet launch | Stage (ETHBEAT) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base | Coinbase | OP Stack, moving to its own codebase | July 2023 | Stage 1 |
| Ink | Kraken | OP Stack | December 2024 | Stage 1 |
| Unichain | Uniswap Labs | OP Stack | February 2025 | Stage 1 |
| World Chain | World | OP Stack | October 2024 | Not yet ranked |
| Soneium | Sony | OP Stack | January 2025 | Not yet ranked |
| Celo | Celo | OP Stack | Became a Layer 2 in March 2025 | Stage 0 |
| Robinhood Chain | Robinhood | Arbitrum | July 2026 | Not yet ranked |

"Not yet ranked" means ETHBEAT doesn't yet consider it a rollup that qualifies for Stage 0, for example because its proof system isn't fully running, or because only a few parties are allowed to challenge. In 2026, Base announced it would leave the OP Stack for its own codebase, staying compatible during the transition.

**Announced, not confirmed to be running:**

- **DAMA 2** from Deutsche Bank, a tokenization platform on a chain built with the ZK Stack. The June 2025 press release targeted a first release in the second half of 2025.
- **Nightfall**, developed by EY, an open-source rollup that keeps transactions private using zero-knowledge proofs. In 2026 a deployment on Starknet was announced; no mainnet confirmation has been seen yet.
- **Jovay** from Ant Digital (Ant Group). It had a testnet in 2025, but ETHBEAT currently lists it as archived.

**Shut down:** Kinto, a Layer 2 only for identity-verified users that had reached Stage 1, closed on September 30, 2025. A dedicated network can shut down; plan ahead for how users will withdraw their assets.

## Maturity: Stage 0, 1, 2

ETHBEAT ranks rollups at three stages:

- **Stage 0:** data and state are posted to Ethereum, anyone can reconstruct the state, and there is a proof system. The operator still has a lot of power.
- **Stage 1:** only someone who controls at least 75% of a **security council** can block withdrawals or push through invalid ones. Upgrades made by anyone else must give users at least 7 days to withdraw first.
- **Stage 2:** anyone can submit a fraud proof, users have at least 30 days to withdraw before an upgrade they don't want, and the security council may step in only when a bug is detected onchain.

On November 8, 2026, ETHBEAT tracked 95 networks: 6 at Stage 1 (Base, Arbitrum One, OP Mainnet, Ink, Starknet, Unichain) and 4 at Stage 2, all very small. Most enterprise Layer 2s are still at Stage 0 or not yet ranked. That means users still have to trust the operator more than Ethereum itself requires.

## Summary

- Layer 2s give businesses speed, low fees and customization, while still recording results on Ethereum.
- Sharing an existing network is simple; running your own gives you customization and the fees, in exchange for operating it and asking customers to trust you.
- Coinbase, Kraken, Uniswap Labs, Sony, World and Robinhood all have their own networks. Most enterprise networks have not reached Stage 1.
