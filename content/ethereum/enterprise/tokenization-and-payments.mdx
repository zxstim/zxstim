**Tokenization** means recording ownership of an asset, such as fund units, bonds or deposits, as [tokens](../basics/using-ethereum/tokens-stablecoins-nfts.mdx) on a blockchain. Tokens can move 24/7, settle in a few minutes instead of a few days, and can carry rules that enforce themselves, for example transfers only to verified investors.

## Running on Ethereum today

Figures change quickly, so every number comes with a date.

- **Stablecoins:** about 135.3 billion USD on Ethereum and 15.67 billion USD on Layer 2s (DefiLlama, via ethereum.org, November 8, 2026). Most of it is USDT and USDC.
- **Tokenized real-world assets:** about 15.2 billion USD (Ethereum Foundation, March 2026).
- **Named examples** (according to ethereum.org and the Ethereum Foundation):
  - BlackRock's BUIDL fund, issued through Securitize.
  - The European Investment Bank (EIB)'s digital bonds, first issued in 2021. These issues still used traditional legal documentation and licensed intermediaries.
  - The EURCV stablecoin from SG-Forge (part of Société Générale).
  - PayPal's PYUSD stablecoin and Circle's USDC.
  - Tokenized money market funds from JPMorgan and from Amundi.
- **Pilots:** Visa's tokenized asset platform (VTAP), which lets banks issue their own ERC-20 tokens.

<Callout type="info" title="Note">
Some frequently cited examples don't actually run on Ethereum. The Monetary Authority of Singapore's trial foreign exchange trade with J.P. Morgan (2022) ran through Aave on Polygon. Siemens' digital bond (2023) was also issued on Polygon, a separate network with its own validators. Always ask: which network does it run on?
</Callout>

## Stablecoin payments

Strengths:

- **Fast and always on.** Transactions are confirmed in a few seconds on Layer 2 and reach finality after about 15 minutes on Ethereum, weekends included.
- **Low fees,** especially on Layer 2. See [Gas fees](../basics/getting-started/gas-fees.mdx).
- **No chargebacks.** Buyers can't claw back money they've paid the way they can with a credit card dispute.

What you should know:

- **The issuer can freeze the tokens** at an address. See [Tokens, stablecoins and NFTs](../basics/using-ethereum/tokens-stablecoins-nfts.mdx).
- **Your revenue may be visible.** If you use one receiving address for every customer, anyone can see your total revenue, competitors included. See [Privacy](../basics/using-ethereum/privacy.mdx).
- **Pick the right network.** USDC on Ethereum and USDC on a Layer 2 live on two different networks. Sending to the wrong network can lose you the money.
- **Holding the keys is on you.** Who holds the keys (one person, a multisig, or a custodian) is the business's own operational risk. See [Smart wallets](../basics/using-ethereum/smart-wallets.mdx).
- **Accounting, taxes and converting to Vietnamese dong** are still real costs. The payment figures on ethereum.org's small business page are US figures.

## In Vietnam

The legal framework is still being built:

- The Law on Digital Technology Industry (No. 71/2025/QH15, in effect from January 1, 2026) defines digital assets, including crypto assets, for the first time.
- Resolution No. 05/2025/NQ-CP (September 9, 2025) pilots a crypto asset market for 5 years.

Using crypto assets or stablecoins for payments, bookkeeping and tax filing should be checked against the current regulations of the State Bank of Vietnam and the Ministry of Finance before you do it. This wiki does not claim that anything is legal or illegal.

## Summary

- Tokenization records asset ownership onchain: transfers 24/7, settlement in minutes, with rules that enforce themselves.
- Ethereum already holds more than 135 billion USD in stablecoins, along with tokenized funds and bonds from major institutions (figures from November 2026).
- Risks: issuers can freeze tokens, revenue may be visible, choosing the wrong network, key custody, and Vietnam's regulations are still changing.
