If your payment or data infrastructure is run by a competitor, you depend on their goodwill: their fees, their terms, who gets priority. Ethereum is different. The room in each block for transactions, called **blockspace**, belongs to no one. Whoever pays the fee can use it, under the same rules, whether a big bank or a small shop, in any country.

## No one owns it

- **Thousands of nodes all over the world.** According to Ethernodes (November 2026), there are 8,717 execution layer nodes in 95 countries and territories. Anyone can run a node. See [Nodes](../basics/how-it-works/nodes.mdx).
- **Block creators take turns at random** among a very large number of independent [validators](../basics/how-it-works/validators.mdx).
- **Many independent software clients.** The execution layer has five main clients written by different teams. No single team can quietly change the rules.
- **No "off switch."** The protocol has no admin panel for freezing accounts or reversing transactions. The Ethereum Foundation itself states plainly that it does not operate or control Ethereum.
- **It has never stopped producing blocks** since July 30, 2015.

This property is called **credible neutrality**: the rules favor no one, and anyone can check for themselves that the rules favor no one.

## Why businesses need this

- **Competitors can't change the rules of the game.** On a chain run by a consortium, the big members usually have the big say. On Ethereum, no member has special rights.
- **The same infrastructure as your partners and customers worldwide.** Assets and applications on Ethereum are **composable** with each other. On November 8, 2026, there was about 135.3 billion USD in stablecoins on Ethereum (DefiLlama, via ethereum.org).
- **No dependence on one country's policy.** In 2022, after the United States sanctioned a contract on Ethereum, most blocks were built through filtering relays, but the filtered transactions still made it onto the chain, just more slowly. Details in [Neutral infrastructure no great power owns](../government/global-neutrality.mdx).
- **No dependence on one vendor.** The infrastructure keeps running even if some company goes out of business or changes its prices.

## Layer 2 and neutrality

[Layer 2](./layer-2-for-enterprise.mdx) is a popular way for businesses to use Ethereum with low fees and lots of customization. But a Layer 2 has an **operator**:

- If you run the Layer 2, your customers and partners have to trust you on some points, such as transaction ordering and contract upgrades.
- If a competitor runs the Layer 2, you have to trust the competitor.
- The middle ground: a Layer 2 that posts its data to Ethereum, has proofs and has an **escape hatch** lets users withdraw their assets back to Ethereum even if the operator won't cooperate. See [Verify instead of trust](./verifiability.mdx).

According to ethereum.org's Layer 2 networks page (based on ETHBEAT, formerly L2BEAT; read on November 9, 2026), no network on the list has reached the highest maturity stage yet.

## Limits

- **The layers on top have owners.** Stablecoin issuers can freeze tokens. Layer 2 operators, exchanges and custody services are subject to the laws of the country where they are registered. Neutrality at the protocol layer does not automatically carry over to these layers.
- **Nodes are concentrated in a few countries:** the United States has about 30%, Germany about 14% (Ethernodes, November 2026).
- **One client runs nearly half the network:** Geth runs on about 50% of execution layer nodes (ethereum.org, May 2026). A serious bug there would hit many nodes at once.
- **Filtering relays still exist.** According to MEV Watch, on November 7, 2026, about 16.7% of block deliveries through MEV-Boost relays came from relays that filter transactions against the US sanctions list.
- **In-protocol censorship-resistance guarantees are still in progress.** The FOCIL upgrade (EIP-7805) will force each block to include pending transactions that a group of validators has published. FOCIL has been scheduled for the Hegotá upgrade, but as of November 2026 it has no date.

## Summary

- Ethereum's blockspace belongs to no one: whoever pays the fee can use it, under the same rules.
- Businesses get infrastructure whose rules competitors can't change, and that doesn't depend on one country or one vendor.
- Layer 2s and stablecoins have operators and issuers. Know exactly whom you are trusting at which layer.
