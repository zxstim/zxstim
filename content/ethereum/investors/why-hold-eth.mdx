According to Vitalik, some "mass adoption" goals point very much in the same direction as treating ETH as a store of value. The most important one: the more assets are issued directly on Ethereum, the more you can do with your ETH.

## Swap for other assets, no middleman needed

Ethereum already has:

- **Stablecoins:** about 134.3 billion USD, or 46% of all stablecoins across every chain (DefiLlama, November 9, 2026).
- **Tokenized real-world assets** (funds, bonds): about 15.1 billion USD (rwa.xyz, November 8, 2026). One example is BlackRock's BUIDL fund. See [Tokenization and payments](../enterprise/tokenization-and-payments.mdx).
- **Decentralized assets native** to Ethereum, such as application tokens.

You swap back and forth between ETH and these assets using [smart contracts](../basics/how-it-works/smart-contracts.mdx), without opening an account with an intermediary. See [Tokens, stablecoins and NFTs](../basics/using-ethereum/tokens-stablecoins-nfts.mdx).

## Use it as collateral

ETH is the most widely used collateral in **decentralized finance** (DeFi). For example, on Aave you can deposit ETH (as WETH) to borrow stablecoins without selling your ETH. About 43.1 billion USD sits in DeFi applications on Ethereum, which is 53% of the total across all chains (DefiLlama, November 9, 2026).

Borrowing against collateral carries risk: if ETH's price falls sharply, your collateral can be sold automatically (liquidation).

## Stake to earn rewards

ETH is used to secure the network, and people who stake it are rewarded. ethereum.org calls ETH a store of value and an asset that earns yield through staking.

- **Reward rate:** about 2.4 to 2.5% a year in consensus rewards (validatorqueue.com, November 9, 2026), plus priority fees and MEV. The rate gradually falls as more people stake.
- **Ways to take part:**
  - stake on your own at home;
  - stake through a staking service;
  - use **liquid staking**: you get back a token that represents your staked ETH.
  - See [Validators](../basics/how-it-works/validators.mdx).
- **Risks:** penalties if the validator misbehaves, plus contract risk and operator risk when you use a service. See [Risks and other views](./risks-and-counterviews.mdx).

## Pay fees

ETH is what you use to pay [gas fees](../basics/getting-started/gas-fees.mdx) on Ethereum and on most Layer 2s.

## Not without conflicts

Vitalik acknowledges that serving these uses can still clash with decentralization in places. For example, faster blocks are convenient for trading, but they can make running a validator harder for ordinary people, and lead to centralization. Even so, this conflict is much smaller than on the fee-maximizing path.

## Summary

- The more assets are issued on Ethereum, the more useful ETH becomes: stablecoins, real-world assets, decentralized assets.
- ETH can be swapped for other assets without a middleman, used as collateral, staked to earn rewards of about 2.4 to 2.5% a year, and used to pay fees.
- Each use has its own risks: liquidation when borrowing, and penalties and operator risk when staking.
