## The fee-burning mechanism

Since the London upgrade (August 2021), every transaction pays a fee in two parts. The **base fee** is **burned**, meaning it is removed from the total supply. The **priority fee** goes to the validator. The busier the network, the higher the base fee, and the more ETH gets burned. See [Gas](../basics/how-it-works/gas.mdx).

As of November 8, 2026, about 4.64 million ETH had been burned in total (ultrasound.money).

## The theory: valuing ETH by burned fees

This theory treats burned fees like a cash flow paid to token holders, and values the token by its **net present value** (NPV):

**Market cap = burned fees per year ÷ discount rate**

Vitalik's example: a token burns 1 billion USD a year, steadily over the long term, and people treat 1 billion USD today as worth the same as 1.04 billion USD a year from now (a 4% discount rate). Then 1 billion ÷ 4% = 25 billion USD is seen as a natural "floor."

The reasoning: if the market cap fell below 25 billion, someone could buy the token and then sell more than 4% of their holdings every year while their share of ownership stays the same, because the total supply shrinks by that same amount. That would mean buying a stream of 1 USD a year, forever, for less than 25 USD: a bargain the market wouldn't let last for long.

## Why it was never a real floor

The "floor" argument only holds when the cash flow comes from **outside**: it doesn't shrink when the token's price falls.

From 2021 to 2023, Ethereum burned millions of ETH a year. But most of it came from activity **inside** the crypto world: buying and selling NFTs, lending, trading tokens. If ETH falls 10x, the buyers in those activities are also 10x poorer, so the fees they pay fall too. The cash flow shrinks exactly when the price needs it most.

A famous example: the Otherside virtual land sale by Yuga Labs (the company behind the Bored Ape NFTs, a collection of ape pictures), April 30 to May 1, 2022.

- According to Decrypt, about 55,843 ETH (about 157 million USD) was burned for this sale.
- According to Etherscan, 64,219 ETH was burned over the whole weekend, which the press reported as "180 million USD in fees."

## How the burn has changed

| Year | ETH burned (estimate) | Newly issued ETH (estimate) |
|---|---|---|
| 2021 (from August) | about 1.32 million | |
| 2022 | about 1.47 million | |
| 2023 | about 1.09 million | about 0.75 million |
| 2024 | about 0.65 million | about 0.93 million |
| 2025 | about 97 thousand | about 962 thousand |
| 2026 (through November 7) | about 20 thousand | about 884 thousand |

Figures are calculated from Coin Metrics data and match ultrasound.money's totals to within about 0.4%. The issuance column is left blank for 2021 and 2022 because before The Merge (September 2022), ETH was still issued to miners under a different mechanism.

The burn has dropped sharply since 2024 because most transactions moved to [Layer 2](../basics/how-it-works/layer-2.mdx), where fees are much cheaper, and because blobs made it very cheap for Layer 2s to post their data. In 2025, the burn was only about 10% of new issuance. In the 30 days up to November 8, 2026, that figure was about 3%.

## What about today?

Vitalik acknowledges that Ethereum has better outside support today: the economy of tokenized real-world assets (about 15.1 billion USD on Ethereum, according to rwa.xyz on November 8, 2026) and stablecoins. But precisely because these are normal, steady businesses, they won't suddenly burn 180 million USD in a day the way the 2022 virtual land sale did.

The conclusion: EIP-1559 is still good and should stay, because it makes fees predictable and returns part of the value to everyone who holds ETH. But burned fees shouldn't be treated as the foundation of ETH's value.

## Summary

- The NPV theory treats burned fees as a cash flow and claims they create a floor for the price.
- That floor isn't real, because most burned fees come from activity inside crypto, which shrinks when ETH's price falls.
- The burn has fallen from about 1.47 million ETH (2022) to about 97,000 ETH (2025), mainly because transactions moved to Layer 2.
