Suppose we wanted burned fees to be enough to justify ETH's current market cap of about 270 billion USD (according to Vitalik, November 2026). Then Ethereum would have to operate like one of the world's largest corporations. According to Vitalik, it would rank around L'Oréal, the world's 55th-largest company by market cap.

So what does a large corporation have to do to hold its position over the long run?

## 1. A corporation has to spend

By an estimate Vitalik cites, L'Oréal spends about 0.58% of its market cap on research and development each year. Apply that figure to Ethereum:

- The Ethereum Foundation would run out of money almost immediately.
- The protocol itself would have to set aside about 700,000 ETH a year for research, along with all the political fights over who gets it.
- The income of [validators](../basics/how-it-works/validators.mdx) would be cut by about two-thirds.

And that's only research. A corporation also has to spend on all sorts of other things besides making more of its existing products.

## 2. A corporation has to be run

To survive new waves of technology and culture, a corporation has to keep making bold choices: going after new markets, building new products for people who think in new ways. Ethereum would need a governing body that intervenes deeply, and for the long term.

Both big spending and interventionist governance run counter to decentralization and **capture resistance**: the more money and power is concentrated in one place, the more there is for some group to seize.

## 3. Fees have to be high

Maximizing burned fees means, to some degree, maximizing the gas price. Vitalik points to a basic fact of economics:

- The price that maximizes **price times usage** is always higher than
- the price that gives **the most people** access within the network's safety limits.

In other words, chasing fee revenue pushes some users out. Among them are the people who need privacy and security most, the very people Ethereum was built to serve.

## Comparing the two paths

| | Maximize burned fees | Optimize for store of value |
|---|---|---|
| Ethereum is like | A corporation | Neutral infrastructure, like gold or the Internet |
| It needs | Big spending, heavy-handed governance | A simple, stable protocol with little politics |
| Fees | The higher, the better for revenue | The cheaper, the more people can use it |
| ETH holders need | A leader who is quick to follow trends | A chain that can survive for decades |

According to Vitalik, if you want ETH to chase every financial trend to collect fees, you need a "brave leader" who keeps announcing that Ethereum will change itself to embrace the latest opportunity. If you hold ETH as a store of value, you need the opposite.

## This doesn't mean dropping EIP-1559

The fee-burning mechanism stays. It makes fees predictable and returns part of the value to everyone who holds ETH. What changes is the way of looking at it: it's fine if the burn stays below 10% of the break-even level forever, because ETH's value comes from somewhere else.

## Summary

- To live off fees, Ethereum would have to spend like a corporation (about 700,000 ETH a year for research alone), be run like a corporation, and keep fees high.
- All three go against decentralization and capture resistance.
- The fee-burning mechanism stays, but it isn't the foundation of ETH's value.
