If you hold ETH to chase trends, you'll measure it by revenue and growth rate. If you hold ETH as a **store of value**, you'll measure it by different criteria. Below are the criteria Vitalik lists, along with what Ethereum is doing for each one, as of November 2026.

Status labels: **Live** (running on mainnet), **Scheduled** (chosen for an upgrade, no date yet), **In progress** (research or testnets).

## 1. Survive

The first and most important criterion: survival. According to Vitalik, the most recent Devcon discussed this a great deal, under the theme "post-quantum and post-AI."

- **Post-quantum security.** A powerful enough quantum computer could break the kinds of signatures Ethereum uses today. Since January 2026, the Ethereum Foundation has had a dedicated team for this. New signatures based only on hash functions are being tested on weekly development networks, with more than 10 client teams taking part. The target is around 2029. ethereum.org considers the threat not yet imminent. **In progress.**
- **Choosing the conservative path.** Vitalik wants a chain that relies only on hash functions for its security, is **formally verified** (proven mathematically to run correctly), and uses at least 3 times as many computation rounds as the level known to be safe enough. Formal verification projects are being funded. **In progress.**
- **Moving toward ossification.** Once the core is complete, most development moves to the layers above, and the protocol rarely changes anymore. According to a presentation published on ethereum.org, Ethereum can only ossify once it is post-quantum secure. **Only a direction so far.**
- **Little politics in the protocol.** Set a very high bar before adding things to the protocol that make it bloated, or new rewards that some group could capture.

## 2. Easy to keep safe

- **Multisig wallets and smart wallets.** The ERC-4337 standard has been running since March 2023, and EIP-7702 has given regular accounts smart wallet features since May 2025. See [Smart wallets](../basics/using-ethereum/smart-wallets.mdx). **Live.**
- **Frame transactions** (EIP-8141): bring features like recovery and having someone else pay your fees directly into the protocol, and make it possible to switch to post-quantum signatures. **Scheduled** for the Hegotá upgrade.

## 3. Keep it private

- **Kohaku:** an open-source toolkit that lets wallets add privacy features. See [Privacy](../basics/using-ethereum/privacy.mdx). **In progress.**

## 4. Private and safe at the same time

These two often pull against each other: privacy tools are often hard to use together with multisig wallets or recoverable wallets.

- **EIP-8288** (draft, June 2026): combines the signatures and proofs of many transactions into a single proof per block. That makes post-quantum signatures and privacy proofs cheap enough for everyday use. **In progress.**
- Work on "private account abstraction" has also begun. **In progress.**

## 5. If you have the coins, you can send them

If you have ETH, you must be able to send it, and no block builder should be able to stop you.

- Today: when some builders filter transactions, those transactions still make it onto the chain through builders that don't filter, just more slowly. See [Neutral infrastructure](../government/global-neutrality.mdx).
- **FOCIL** (EIP-7805) builds this guarantee into the protocol itself: a randomly chosen group of validators publishes the pending transactions, and any block that leaves them out is rejected. **Scheduled** for Hegotá, no date yet.

## 6. Little dependence on infrastructure

You shouldn't have to trust a company to know your own balance.

- **Running your own node on ordinary hardware.**
  - According to ethereum.org, the minimum spec is 2 cores, 16 GB of RAM, a 2 TB NVMe drive and a 25 Mbit/s connection.
  - Since July 2025, nodes no longer have to keep old history, which saves 300 to 500 GB.
  - Vitalik says the node on his laptop finished syncing in under 6 hours and takes up only an eighth of the disk, better than even in 2019.
  - See [Nodes](../basics/how-it-works/nodes.mdx). **Live.**
- **Statelessness** (nodes that don't need to keep the full state), so that even phones can verify things for themselves. **In progress.**

## Summary

- The number one criterion for a store of value is survival: post-quantum security, choosing the conservative path, moving toward ossification, and little politics.
- Next come staying safe, staying private, being private and safe at once, always being able to send, and not depending on anyone's infrastructure.
- Some of this is live (smart wallets, running your own node on ordinary hardware). Most of it is scheduled or in progress.
