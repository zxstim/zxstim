## Prices are extremely volatile

ETH's biggest drops, based on daily closing prices (Coin Metrics):

| From | To | Drop |
|---|---|---|
| January 2018 | December 2018 | about 94% |
| November 2021 | June 2022 | about 79% |
| August 22, 2025 (4,831 USD) | June 6, 2026 (1,565 USD) | about 68% |

On November 7, 2026, ETH closed at 2,234 USD, about 54% below its highest close. A store of value is judged over many decades, but in the short term ETH is still far more volatile than gold or large-company stocks.

## Being a store of value is a position that has to be earned

An asset only stores value when enough people believe it does, for long enough. ETH has to compete with gold, with bitcoin and with other assets. Vitalik's argument explains why Ethereum has an advantage, but it doesn't guarantee the market will agree.

## Staking risks

- **Penalties** when a validator misbehaves, even because of a configuration mistake. If many validators misbehave at the same time, the penalty can reach the entire stake.
- **Liquid staking** adds smart contract risk, the risk that the representative token loses value relative to ETH, governance risk, and the risk of concentration in a few operators. When penalties hit, the token holders share the loss.
- **Concentration:** Lido alone holds about 21 to 22% of all staked ETH (according to Lido's own report for the first half of 2026, and onchain data from November 2026). The Ethereum Foundation's "Trillion Dollar Security" report considers the dominance of large staking pools a risk.
- ethereum.org notes that staking services can become targets for attackers or for regulators.

## Technical risks

- **Smart contract bugs:** buggy code, misused upgrade permissions, code that hasn't been audited.
- **Quantum computers:** according to ethereum.org, the threat isn't imminent, and work on switching to safe signatures is underway. See [What ETH should be measured by](./store-of-value-criteria.mdx).
- **Lost keys, scams:** most of the money lost in crypto is lost because users get scammed or lose their keys, not because of the protocol. See [Staying safe](../basics/getting-started/staying-safe.mdx).

## Legal

Rules on crypto assets differ from country to country and change quickly. In Vietnam, the Law on Digital Technology Industry (No. 71/2025/QH15) and Resolution No. 05/2025/NQ-CP on piloting a crypto asset market lay the first groundwork. Check them against the rules currently in force, including on taxes.

## Other views

Not everyone agrees that burned fees don't matter.

- **The "ultrasound money" camp.** Justin Drake, an Ethereum Foundation researcher and the person who coined the "ultrasound money" slogan, argues that ETH needs less issuance or more burning so that total supply shrinks again (remarks from February 2025). The Bankless podcast once called ETH a "three-in-one" asset: a consumable commodity (paying fees), a yield-bearing asset (staking) and a store of value.
- **ethereum.org emphasizes a different role.** Its page comparing Ethereum with Bitcoin says ETH is both fuel and a store of value, but that its main role is to power an entire ecosystem.
- **Total supply is growing.** New issuance is currently about 0.89% a year, while the burn is only a few percent of that. People who see scarcity as a requirement for a store of value will see this as a weakness. People who follow Vitalik's argument consider stable, public issuance rules more important. See [ETH supply and security](./supply-and-security.mdx).

## Summary

- ETH has fallen 94%, 79% and 68% in its major downturns. Being a store of value is a position that has to be earned, not a given.
- Staking carries penalty risk, contract risk and concentration risk. Lido holds about one-fifth of staked ETH.
- There are other views: the "ultrasound money" camp values burned fees, and ethereum.org emphasizes ETH's role in powering the ecosystem.
