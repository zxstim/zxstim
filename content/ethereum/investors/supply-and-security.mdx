## Supply

- **In circulation:** 122,200,838 ETH (ultrasound.money, November 8, 2026).
- **No fixed cap.** ethereum.org states clearly that Ethereum has no supply cap, unlike bitcoin. According to ethereum.org, this design ensures the network always has a budget to pay for its security.

## New issuance

- **Before The Merge** (September 2022): about 13,000 ETH a day for miners and validators, or about 4.6% of total supply a year.
- **After The Merge:** only validators receive new ETH. Issuance grows with the square root of the total amount of ETH staked: four times as much staked means only twice as much issued.
- **Today:** about 2,990 ETH a day, which is about 1.09 million ETH a year, or about 0.89% of total supply (ultrasound.money, November 8, 2026).

## Burned ETH

- Since August 2021, the base fee of every transaction has been burned. About 4.64 million ETH has been burned in total, 2.02 million of it since The Merge (ultrasound.money, November 8, 2026).
- The yearly burn has dropped sharply since 2024, as transactions moved to Layer 2. See [Burned fees were never a price floor](./burned-fees-are-not-a-floor.mdx).

## Putting it together: how total supply has changed

From The Merge to November 8, 2026, total supply grew from 120,521,141 to 122,200,838 ETH: about 1.68 million ETH more, or 1.39%, which is about 0.33% a year.

- In 2023, more ETH was burned than issued, so total supply shrank. That was the era of the "**ultrasound money**" slogan, which ethereum.org calls a community meme about ETH's total supply possibly shrinking over time.
- Since 2024, issuance has outpaced the burn, so total supply is slowly growing again.

From the [store of value](./eth-as-store-of-value.mdx) point of view, what matters isn't whether total supply shrinks or grows slightly, but that the issuance rules are stable, public, and can't be changed by anyone at will.

## Security: staked ETH

- **Currently staked:** about 44.5 million ETH, or 36.39% of total supply, with 814,340 active validators (validatorqueue.com, November 9, 2026). See [Validators](../basics/how-it-works/validators.mdx).
- **Finality:** after about 15 minutes, a transaction can't be reversed unless an attacker is willing to lose a very large amount of money. See [Consensus](../basics/how-it-works/consensus.mdx).

How large a share of staked ETH an attacker needs, according to ethereum.org:

| Share of staked ETH | What it lets an attacker do |
|---|---|
| 33% | Delay finality |
| 34% | Make two competing blocks both reach finality |
| 51% | Censor transactions |
| 66% | Rewrite history that has already reached finality |

Reversing a finalized block would cause at least one-third of all staked ETH to be burned: nearly 15 million ETH, based on November 2026 figures.

**Slashing** (penalties for cheating): a validator that cheats immediately loses a small part of its stake, then after 18 days receives an additional penalty that grows with the number of validators that cheated along with it. If a great many cheated together, the penalty can reach the entire stake.

## Long-term security goals

The Ethereum Foundation's "Trillion Dollar Security" report (May 2025) sets two goals:

- billions of people, each comfortably holding more than 1,000 USD onchain;
- institutions comfortably holding more than 1 trillion USD in a single contract.

The report splits the work into six areas: user experience, smart contracts, infrastructure and cloud, consensus, incident response, and governance. It also names the weak points openly, such as the dominance of a few large staking pools and the threat from quantum computers.

## Summary

- About 122.2 million ETH is in circulation, with no fixed cap. New issuance is about 0.89% a year, and the burn is currently only a few percent of that.
- Since The Merge, total supply has grown about 0.33% a year.
- About 44.5 million ETH (36%) is staked. To reverse finalized history, an attacker would have to accept losing at least one-third of it.
