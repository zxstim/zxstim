Why does ETH have value? Two answers come up a lot.

- **ETH is a token that pays "dividends" through burned fees.** Every transaction burns some ETH (see [Gas fees](../basics/getting-started/gas-fees.mdx)). The more people use Ethereum, the more ETH gets burned, and the more ETH holders benefit. In this view, ETH is like a company's stock, and its value comes from cash flow.
- **ETH is a store of value** (SoV): something people hold to preserve their wealth over time, like gold. Its value comes from people believing it will still be there, safe, with no one able to take it from them, many decades from now.

On November 8, 2026, Vitalik Buterin, a co-founder of Ethereum, wrote that we should focus on the second view. This section explains that argument, with figures from ethereum.org and independent sources.

## The argument in three points

1. **Burned fees were never as solid a support as they looked.** Most of the fees burned from 2021 to 2023 came from activity inside the crypto world. When ETH's price falls, that activity shrinks with it, so it can't serve as a "price floor." See [Burned fees were never a price floor](./burned-fees-are-not-a-floor.mdx).
2. **Maximizing burned fees would come at the cost of decentralization.** To live off fees like a corporation, Ethereum would have to spend like a corporation, be run like a corporation, and keep fees high. All three go against what gives Ethereum its value. See [The cost of maximizing fees](./cost-of-maximizing-fees.mdx).
3. **Being a store of value points in the same direction as Ethereum's strengths:** decentralization and **CROPS** (censorship resistance and capture resistance, open source, privacy, security). See [Decentralization, CROPS and stability](./crops-and-stability.mdx).

Vitalik is clear: the EIP-1559 fee-burning mechanism is still good and should stay. But there's no need to worry if the burn stays below 10% of the break-even level forever, because ETH's value comes from somewhere else.

## The core idea: stability

What people value most about Ethereum is decentralization and CROPS. The biggest practical benefit they bring is **stability**: the rules don't change at anyone's whim, no one can freeze your assets, and the network doesn't depend on any single company or country.

For many other applications, these properties may be merely "nice to have," pursued out of idealism. A store of value is different: they are exactly what people entrusting it with their wealth need most. And according to Vitalik, in an age when artificial intelligence is upending so much, stability will be valued even more.

## Not just one person's opinion

The Ethereum Foundation's mandate (March 2026), published on ethereum.org, also:

- treats CROPS as a non-negotiable condition for all development on Ethereum;
- states that ether is a store of value and is money;
- sets out the **walkaway test**: the protocol must keep working even if the Ethereum Foundation and the core developers disappear.

ethereum.org's "What is ETH" page calls ETH a store of value and an asset that earns yield through [staking](./why-hold-eth.mdx).

## Vitalik's conclusion

- **The best social reason to care about Ethereum:** it brings decentralization and CROPS to more people, for more uses.
- **The best personal reason to hold ETH:** its store-of-value properties, which are a direct result of decentralization and CROPS.

<Callout type="warn" title="Important">
This is an argument to help you understand, not investment advice. This wiki doesn't predict prices. ETH has lost more than 90% of its value within a single year. See [Risks and other views](./risks-and-counterviews.mdx).
</Callout>

## Summary

- There are two ways to look at ETH's value: a token that lives off burned fees, or a store of value.
- Vitalik Buterin (November 2026) argues the second is more accurate: burned fees were never a solid support, maximizing fees goes against decentralization, and being a store of value points in the same direction as CROPS.
- The core idea: decentralization and CROPS bring stability, and stability is what people holding assets need most.
