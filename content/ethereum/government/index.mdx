This section is for civil servants, policymakers, regulators and researchers in the public sector. You don't need to know how to code. If you don't yet know what a blockchain is, read the [Basics](../basics/index.mdx) section first.

What makes Ethereum worth a look for the public sector is its **global neutrality**: a shared infrastructure that no government, corporation or foundation can, on its own, switch off, change, or use to block another country.

## Reading order

1. [Ethereum from the public sector's view](./ethereum-for-the-public-sector.mdx): a public record book with no owning agency. What it does well, and what it can't do.
2. [Neutral infrastructure no great power owns](./global-neutrality.mdx): why no single party can control it, the 2022 test, and the limits of neutrality.
3. [What goes onchain, what stays offchain](./what-goes-onchain.mdx): what goes onchain is proof, not personal data.
4. [Digital identity and credentials](./identity-and-credentials.mdx): Bhutan, Singapore, and digital documents in citizens' wallets.
5. [Financial and public data transparency](./financial-and-data-transparency.mdx): trials in Canada, the United States, Kenya, Rwanda and at UNICEF.
6. [Use-case ideas, not projects yet](./use-case-ideas.mdx): six suggested directions, with hard questions for each.
7. [Evaluating Ethereum](./evaluating-options.mdx): public chain, private chain or Layer 2.
8. [Risks and accountability](./risks-and-accountability.mdx): keys, fees, legal validity, personal data, and pilots that were stopped.
9. [Working with the Ethereum Foundation, and references](./working-with-the-ef.mdx)

## Sources for this section

Most of this content is summarized from the Ethereum Foundation's (EF) public-sector site and from ethereum.org, along with figures from independent sources. The EF advocates for Ethereum, so this wiki keeps a neutral tone: it also covers projects that were stopped, examples that actually run on other networks, and things the original material rarely mentions. Each page has a "Sources" section so you can check for yourself.
