On Ethereum, every transfer leaves a public trail: how much, from which address, to which address, and when. For public funds and aid money, that lets outsiders follow things for themselves instead of waiting for a report. See [Reading a transaction](../basics/using-ethereum/reading-a-transaction.mdx).

The examples below come from the Ethereum Foundation's (EF) list of case studies. Of the 17 cases the EF lists, only 4 are from national government agencies (Bhutan, Singapore, Canada, the United States). The rest were carried out by United Nations agencies or non-governmental organizations.

## Canada: publishing grants (2018 to 2019, stopped)

- The Industrial Research Assistance Program (IRAP) of Canada's National Research Council published its grants, along with every amendment, through a smart contract on Ethereum mainnet.
- The pilot ran from January 2018 to March 1, 2019. According to the results report, the publication page had more than 162,000 views from more than 200 countries.
- There is no information on it being put into regular operation after the pilot.

## United States: economic data

- On August 28, 2025, the Department of Commerce published the hash of its GDP report on 9 blockchain networks, including Ethereum. See [What goes onchain](./what-goes-onchain.mdx).
- Separately from that, two **oracle** companies (services that bring outside data onto a blockchain), Chainlink and Pyth, supply figures such as GDP to smart contracts. These feeds were still running in Q3 2026. The data belongs to the government, but the path it takes onto the chain depends on private companies.

## Kenya: cash support ahead of drought

- Carried out by non-governmental organizations, not the government: Prosper Global Ventures (formerly Mercy Corps Ventures) and DIVA Donate.
- How it works: a smart contract tracks a vegetation index measured by satellite. When the index hits the drought threshold, money is paid out automatically in the RLUSD stablecoin (issued by Ripple), and reaches people through M-Pesa, the popular mobile money service in Kenya.
- Results from October to December 2025, reported by the project itself and not independently verified: 4 of 9 checks triggered a payout, about 16,000 USD was paid out, money reached recipients within 1 to 5.5 hours, and costs were 3.6% against a 10% benchmark.
- One incident: during the US government shutdown, the satellite data feed was interrupted and one payout was delayed by 18 days.
- Network: the 2023 trial ran on Polygon, a separate network with its own validators. The 2025 expansion ran on Ethereum; the documentation doesn't say whether on mainnet or a Layer 2.

## UNICEF and Giga: receiving and using crypto

- **UNICEF's CryptoFund** has received and granted funds in crypto since 2019, more than 4 million USD in total, including 2,602 ETH. All ETH transfers out of it can be tracked on Ethereum.
- **Giga** is an initiative of UNICEF and the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) to connect schools to the internet. In Rwanda, the EF donated 32 ETH, and a validator run by Giga together with Launchnodes started running in May 2022.
  - [Staking](../basics/how-it-works/validators.mdx) rewards added up to about 5,600 USD over 2022 to 2024, enough to pay one school's internet bill. This is the total for the whole period, not a yearly yield.
  - The pilot ended on April 9, 2025, once the Rwandan government was paying for the internet contracts itself.

## Things to keep in mind

- **Transparent money flows aren't transparent outcomes.** The ledger records where the money went, not whether it was used for the right purpose.
- **The last mile is offchain.** In Kenya, the part where money reaches people goes through M-Pesa, not Ethereum.
- **Stablecoins can be frozen** by their own issuer. See [Tokens, stablecoins and NFTs](../basics/using-ethereum/tokens-stablecoins-nfts.mdx).
- **Most are small pilots.** When you read an example, ask: is the project still running, which network does it run on, and were the results independently evaluated?

## Summary

- Every transfer on Ethereum leaves a public trail, so outsiders can follow money flows for themselves.
- The existing examples are mostly pilots, many run by international or non-governmental organizations. Canada stopped in 2019; Giga in Rwanda ended in 2025.
- Transparent money flows don't replace auditing the outcomes, and the last mile is often offchain.
