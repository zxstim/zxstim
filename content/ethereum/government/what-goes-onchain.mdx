The general rule: **put the proof onchain, keep the data where it is.** According to the Ethereum Foundation (EF), sensitive data can stay in the agency's existing systems. Ethereum only holds what others need to check things for themselves.

## What usually goes onchain

- **Document hashes.** A **hash** is a file's "fingerprint": change a single comma and you get a completely different hash, and no one can rebuild the file from its hash.
- **Lists of recognized issuers.** For example: the schools or agencies allowed to issue a given type of document.
- **Revocation status.** Which degrees or licenses have been revoked.
- **Money flows.** Which payments went out, from where to where, and when. See [Financial and public data transparency](./financial-and-data-transparency.mdx).

## Putting a hash onchain: how it works

1. The agency computes the hash of a file, for example the PDF of a report.
2. The agency sends a transaction containing that hash, signed with the agency's key. The transaction lands in a block, along with a timestamp.
3. Later, anyone with the file can compute the hash again and compare it with the one onchain. A match means the file hasn't been changed since that time, and that it really was published by the agency's address.

The hashes of thousands of documents can be combined into a single hash, so one transaction is enough for a whole batch.

**Example:** on August 28, 2025, the US Department of Commerce published the hash of the PDF of its Q2 2025 GDP figures on 9 blockchain networks, including Ethereum mainnet and two Layer 2 networks, Arbitrum One and OP Mainnet.

<Callout type="warn" title="Important">
Integrity doesn't mean accuracy. A hash proves a document hasn't been changed, not that its content is correct. Figures that were wrong from the start are still wrong once written onchain.
</Callout>

## Why personal data shouldn't go onchain

- **Public and permanent.** On mainnet, anyone can read it, and no one can delete it. See [Privacy](../basics/using-ethereum/privacy.mdx).
- **It clashes with citizens' rights.** The Law on Personal Data Protection (No. 91/2025/QH15, in effect from January 1, 2026) recognizes data subjects' right to correct their data and to request its deletion. Data written to a public chain can't be corrected or deleted. Check your design against the rules in force before you build.
- **Hashes can leak too.** The hash of an easy-to-guess value, such as an ID number or a date of birth, can be cracked by trying every possibility in turn. Don't write hashes of personal data without a security expert designing how it's done.
- **The traces that come with it.** Even if no data is written, timing, frequency and edits can still reveal information.

## Read a lot, write a little

Reading data on Ethereum is free; only writing costs a [gas fee](../basics/how-it-works/gas.mdx). According to the EF, Bhutan's digital identity system mostly reads and very rarely writes.

A Rutgers University study cited by the EF found a median fee in Q1 2026 of about 0.012 USD on Ethereum and about 0.0015 USD on three Layer 2 networks. Fees change with how busy the network is, so check the current figures.

## Summary

- Onchain: hashes, issuer lists, revocation status, money flows. The original data stays in the agency's systems.
- A hash proves a document hasn't been changed since a certain time, not that its content is correct.
- Personal data doesn't go on a public chain: it can't be corrected or deleted, and even its hash can leak it.
