A paper diploma can be forged, and checking it means asking whoever issued it. A **verifiable credential** is a digital document carrying the issuer's signature: anyone who receives it can check it right away, without having to call and ask.

## Three roles: issuer, holder, verifier

- **Issuer**: a school or a licensing agency. The issuer signs the credential with its own key.
- **Holder**: a citizen, who keeps the credential in a wallet on their phone.
- **Verifier**: a bank or an employer. The verifier checks the signature without having to contact the issuer.

Ethereum plays the role of a shared registry anyone can look up: which issuers are recognized, what each type of credential contains, and which credentials have been revoked. Personal data doesn't go onchain. See [Digital signatures](../basics/how-it-works/digital-signatures.mdx) to understand why a signature can be checked without knowing the key.

## Show only what's needed

- **Selective disclosure**: share only the part that's needed. For example: proving you're over 18 without giving your date of birth.
- **Zero-knowledge proof**: proving something is true without revealing the data behind it. An EF prototype (November 2026) can prove that a credential was issued by a recognized issuer and hasn't been revoked, without saying who the issuer is.

Can cheap phones handle it? An EF team ran measurements on 10 Android phones (November 2026). A proof bundling 8 checks took about 8 to 10 seconds on low-end phones, compared with 1.4 to 1.9 seconds on high-end ones. On a phone with 4 GB of RAM, Android killed the initial setup step partway through because it used too much memory. These prototypes only run on testnets so far and have not been independently audited.

## Bhutan: national digital identity

- Bhutan's national digital identity system (NDI) has been running since 2023, originally on the Polygon network. On October 13, 2025, Bhutan announced it was moving NDI to Ethereum mainnet. The EF's public-sector site says a broader migration was planned for early 2026, but doesn't say whether it has been completed.
- On Ethereum: the list of recognized issuers, the definitions of credential types, and **decentralized identifiers** (DIDs). Credentials and personal data live in citizens' NDI wallets.
- At the end of 2025, NDI had 449,549 users (end of 2024: 172,922). According to the annual report of August 19, 2026, more than 70 public services, all 5 of Bhutan's banks and both of its 2 mobile carriers had connected.

## Singapore: OpenCerts diplomas, and why it changed

- Starting in 2018, schools in Singapore recorded the issuing and revoking of diplomas on public Ethereum. Learners' data didn't go onchain. By December 2019, 18 institutions had joined.
- On October 1, 2025, OpenCerts dropped its Ethereum-based model and moved to TrustVC: digitally signed files plus an online status list. Old diplomas can still be verified.
- The lesson: a system that has worked well for years can still change its architecture. From the start, plan for documents already issued to remain verifiable when the system changes.

## United Nations training certificates

The International Training Centre of the International Labour Organization (ITCILO) had issued more than 208,000 digital certificates as of October 2025. The EF's page doesn't say which network the system runs on.

## Things to plan for

- **Lost phones.** Citizens need a way to get their documents back, for example [recovery through relatives or another device](../basics/using-ethereum/smart-wallets.mdx). The page on Bhutan also raises this issue.
- **People without a phone**, or who share one phone with their family.
- **Revocation has a delay.** In the EF prototype, a revoked credential could still pass a check for up to an hour.
- **Legal validity.** Whether digital documents count the same as paper ones is up to the law.

## Summary

- Digital credentials carry the issuer's signature; anyone who receives one can check it for themselves.
- Ethereum holds the public part: which issuers are recognized and which credentials have been revoked. Personal data lives in citizens' wallets.
- Bhutan is moving its national digital identity onto Ethereum. Singapore used Ethereum for diplomas from 2018 to 2025, then switched to another approach.
